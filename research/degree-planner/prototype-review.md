# Review of the schedule-first prototype

I would adopt the schedule as the primary artifact and make transcript-to-full-schedule autocomplete a core product promise. My earlier proposal made the next term the front door and put whole-degree generation later. The schedule-first idea serves someone who wants Flow to do the work on day one and someone who wants to use it like a sheet of paper. The current term is a useful focus when a decision is due. A student planning backwards from a course or a year abroad has just as legitimate a starting point. [Revised product pitch](schedule-first.md) · [Earlier pitch](experience.md)

## What I reviewed

I read the pasted notes, the complete feature record on `alex-mockup-1` at `ddf9647`, and the schedule, generator, requirement checks, report, and tests in that revision. I inspected the sample board, CS 488 graph, and progress report in an offline browser. All 29 existing tests passed. I also exercised the branch’s own model with controlled inputs. These checks used sample or synthetic records. The 25 enabled rules include a fictional demo rule and exclude co-op and PD completion. The older basket-first flow was treated as superseded by the schedule-first proposal. ## What I would keep

- **The term grid as a sheet you can own.** It accepts placements, notes, and variable term targets. A student who already knows the answer can put it down immediately. A useful plan can survive a missing ruleset. Keep a full view for comparing sequences and a smaller window for everyday decisions. The other prototype already demonstrates a term window and an all-terms view. [Alex’s schedule design](https://github.com/trinity-y/uwflow-course-planner-prototype/blob/ddf964795ca4ccfb0fa9d99be8bc879e35484b56/Alex%20mockup.md#schedule-and-course-discovery) · [V2 term window](https://github.com/trinity-y/uwflow-course-planner-prototype/blob/14a9e18ce28c5e16d814d06b8cf4e1e3903427fa/prototype/v2/TermBoard.tsx#L49)
- **“Schedule for Me” is a good interaction.** The student knows the course, delegates its placement, then can drag it into a term to fix the decision. Extend that same principle in the other direction. “One science elective in winter” fixes the term and the purpose while delegating the course. A prerequisite collection can likewise hold acceptable routes to a goal. Only the selected route uses capacity. [Automatic planning brief](https://github.com/trinity-y/uwflow-course-planner-prototype/blob/ddf964795ca4ccfb0fa9d99be8bc879e35484b56/Alex%20mockup.md#automatic-planning)
- **The course-to-rule links are already unusually helpful.** The report distinguishes counted courses from additional eligible choices. A course can link back to several requirements, and prerequisites can be inspected from its card. Preserve that vocabulary. Eligibility for two categories does not establish that one course can fill both. [Report and course drill-through](https://github.com/trinity-y/uwflow-course-planner-prototype/blob/ddf964795ca4ccfb0fa9d99be8bc879e35484b56/prototype/webflow-style/degree-view.mjs#L194)

## How to make choices without a giant decision tree

**Show the difference between the options the student is considering.** The prototype supplies a good example. CS 488 in Spring 2027 needs an earlier choice between CS 370 and CS 371 in the bundled graph. I placed each in Fall 2026 against the same sample schedule. Both cleared CS 488’s immediate warning. CS 370 had no missing modeled prerequisites of its own. CS 371 still needed MATH 235 and MATH 237. That is what the student should see while comparing the two. Otherwise both can look equally viable because the downstream warning cleared. Offering and enrolment checks would still be necessary for a personal recommendation. [Sample prerequisite paths](https://github.com/trinity-y/uwflow-course-planner-prototype/blob/ddf964795ca4ccfb0fa9d99be8bc879e35484b56/prototype/webflow-style/calendar.mjs) · [Prerequisite evaluation](https://github.com/trinity-y/uwflow-course-planner-prototype/blob/ddf964795ca4ccfb0fa9d99be8bc879e35484b56/prototype/webflow-style/model.mjs#L117)

The same comparison works in either direction. From completed courses, offer eligible next choices and show which ones advance a requirement, enable a goal, or satisfy an interest. From a desired future course, show the prerequisite routes back to the student’s record. Surface a branch when it changes a decision the student cares about. A long tree of equivalent substitutions asks for effort without helping them choose.

I would use the solver to answer three different questions. **Can my complete schedule work?** Show an actual route and the assumptions it depends on. **Can I take this alternative instead?** Hold the proposed change and the student’s other commitments fixed, search for a repair, and show its cost in terms or courses moved. **Can I defer this choice?** Try each alternative while keeping intervening decisions fixed. If both remain possible until the next selection window, say so. If one needs action this term, identify the course or available place that creates the deadline. Two separately feasible choices can still conflict when selected together.

A bounded search can find a route. Running out of search cannot establish that graduation is impossible. Even an exact solver answers only for its inputs and encoded rules. I would call the first result a **workable starting schedule** and describe the default priorities. Respect recorded facts, required choices and term limits first. When revising an existing schedule, favour the smallest disruption. Use interests and student reviews to distinguish options where they help. One solver result says little about a student's preferred workload, goals they have yet to name, or which alternatives stay open.

## What to do about special circumstances

| Question | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Can transcript upload really lead straight to a whole plan? | Make it a core acceptance case for a supported program. The main Flow importer already extracts a program name and course codes by term and level. Its summary omits grades and outcomes, and its parser explicitly skips lines it identifies as transfer credits. The branch’s personal sample is seeded from a workbook. Show what the import recognized, ask only for consequential missing facts, and generate a conditional draft if an answer is unknown. [Existing parser](../../flow/api/parse/transcript/transcript.go) · [Personal sample](https://github.com/trinity-y/uwflow-course-planner-prototype/blob/ddf964795ca4ccfb0fa9d99be8bc879e35484b56/Alex%20mockup.md#personal-plan-and-course-outcomes) |
| Does everything need to become a fictional course? | A real external course or generic credit is a fact with units and an applicable recognition decision. A reserved future place is an intention. A milestone is a condition. They can share space on a schedule or progress report without pretending to be the same thing. An awarded 0.5 generic units can increase the unit total while a request to use them for Methods remains pending. [Transfer and approval examples](data-and-coverage.md#irregular-records-are-ordinary-planning-cases) |
| What about uncheckable rules? | Use a manual checklist **at the unresolved condition**. The student can record a pending ruling, an assumption for this scenario, or a reported approval with its scope. Keep the automatic checks everywhere else. Permission to enter a course grants no prerequisite credit. A user choosing to ignore a rule is distinct from an approved waiver. |
| Are flexible term targets sufficient? | Editable course counts make unusual study/work patterns easy to sketch. Degree units, academic-level progression, prerequisites and term-specific approvals may still constrain what fits. Keep calendar term, academic level and preferred load distinguishable. A capacity of four is useful even when one course is a quarter-unit lab. [Examples across the six faculties](research.md#six-waterloo-examples-that-should-shape-the-design) |
| History or multiple schedules? | Both answer real questions. Use a disposable preview for “drop this course,” history for “get back yesterday’s version,” and an explicit named copy for “with exchange” versus “stay here.” Keep one active schedule, shared actual history, and independently editable future choices and credential assumptions. An input correction should identify which saved futures now need attention. |

## What I would test next

Use the same product with three students or fixtures. Give an entering student only a transcript and a supported program. They should get a named plan they could follow and explain which parts are interchangeable. Give an upper-year student an Arts major/minor spreadsheet with a disputed use of credit. They should be able to keep their schedule, apply the rules, and resolve every unrelated choice manually. Give a student in mid-term a course to drop and a later course they care about. They should see the earliest affected decision, whether their finish still works, and the fewest changes found, with the original schedule recoverable.

The first two improvements I would make to the mockup are to protect recorded history and the decision under test, then make generated elective choices explainable and individually replaceable. Those changes exercise the whole-degree promise, manual ownership, and decision support on the same screen. These are product findings from code and sample data. Direct student and advisor testing remains open.
